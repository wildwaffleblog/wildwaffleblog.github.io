"CITY 410" is a five-episode mockumentary about a city that was never on any map. Each episode starts as an ordinary vlog: a morning in the metro, a conversation about small dreams, a tour of what I carry every day, a morning that follows its usual script, a walk through autumn. Somewhere inside those ordinary hours something is wrong, and the series follows it one layer at a time.

It is filmed in Ryazan, in Russian, and the English subtitles are part of the look. Every episode also contains a podcast-style layer built around one idea, and those ideas had been sitting in my notes since last autumn. The picture has a worn VHS texture and visual effects where the story needs them. Some shots contain digitally altered footage: I used generative AI tools for the effects and Claude as orchestrator. The full list is at the bottom of this post.

I planned the series for more than a year. I shot it on weekends and evenings, edited it on weekday mornings and released it week by week through October 2026. All five episodes are in the playlist below. Watch them in order.

If you have not seen it yet, stop here. The block below is spoilers, together with my favorite stories about how the series was made.

**The story.**
The fictional city exists, under real Russian city - Ryazan. It was emptied after an epidemic called the Hunger, followed by a quarantine and an evacuation. The narrator is a vlogger who slowly works out that he was born down there, that he had a twin brother, and that he was carried out of the city as a baby. The number in the title is actually an easter egg and has a refernce to bible and http.

**The layers.**
Episode 1 is a metro vlog and a cassette from Grandma's cupboard. Episode 2 is about dreaming small and a strange thread on a local forum, with a quiet subplot about ventilation structures. Episode 3 starts with everyday carry and slides into the lore through a photo found in a cassette case: a man named A. Vedernikov, "Last shift", Aug. 1997. By episode 5 you can work out that this was the shift when a baby left the city. Episode 4 is a morning that follows the usual script, a phone call that ends too early and a door found in a dream. Its podcast section is about keeping some magic alive by not knowing everything. Episode 5 goes underground.

**The bible.**
Before shooting I wrote a production bible: nine sheets with episode breakdowns, a continuity tracker for every object that travels through the series, a sheet on who knows what, and one I called "viewer knowledge" that tracks how much the audience knows at each step. It let me check the dosage of information minute by minute.

**What got cut.**
Episode 4 lost a planned scene with a grandmother and an envelope. Episode 5 lost its paper documents. A phone call, a door and one long monologue, delivered while the phone battery runs out, took their place. A sign that read "ГЭС-1" (a hydroelectric station) became "ТП-1" (a traction substation), because that is where an electrician from the transport hub would have worked.

**The effects.**
The underground city, the door and the brother were built with AI image and video tools, then matched back onto the original footage pixel by pixel. The city started as stills made with NanoBanana. The door-opening shot, the brother's transformation and the black eyes were video-to-video work in Seedance and Kling. The usual problems: a cavern ceiling the model read as an object seen from outside, blown-out backgrounds, perspective drifting between edits, Cyrillic lettering that came out wrong. The cure was boring: change one thing at a time and lock the framing. The brother went through several designs, among them insect-like limbs, pure black skin and a body made of living cloth. The one rule from day one was no werewolf hands.

**The ending.**
The finale begins with a vlog I never managed to finish, about why we love autumn. It ends on the same thought starting over and breaking off in the middle of a word.

Tools and AI:
- **Visual effects:** NanoBanana Pro, Seedance 2.5 and Kling O1. Always composited back onto footage I shot myself. Episodes with altered footage carry YouTube's altered-content label.
- **Sound design and music:** Suno v6 and Suno v6-wild.
- **Claude Opus (by Anthropic):** my story consultant and sounding board. We brainstormed podcast themes, stress-tested plot changes and continuity, adapted my Russian scripts into English subtitles and refined prompts for the image and video tools.
- I wrote the scripts, shot the footage and edited the series all by myself.
