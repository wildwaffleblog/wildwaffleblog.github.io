Inktober 2026 is done, and it's the second year in a row I've finished all thirty-one days. This year's story is called Under the Altar: the diary of a young novice who arrives at a remote island convent and, one small detail at a time, starts to understand where she has ended up. It is a horror story, but a quiet one. Nothing in it raises its voice.

Last year's story grew by accident, drawing by drawing. This time I planned everything before the first dot: the arc, the lore, the diary entry for every day, and which object from the official prompt list would carry it. Apple, relic, cactus, ram, trophy, tusk. Each prompt became a thing in the convent that the narrator cleans, carries, hides or is handed. The story is told only through those objects and her short entries, written in a calm, polite, grateful voice, even when she has every reason not to be.

Each day started with a reference image of the object, staged like a photograph from a case file against plain white. I generated those with an AI image tool, then redrew every one by hand as stippling: thousands of tiny dots, dense in the shadows and dissolving toward the white. I drew mostly in the mornings, sometimes at lunch, one drawing a day. The technique gave the whole series a dry, smoky, slightly sterile atmosphere that I couldn't have found any other way.

I wrote the entries in Russian and translated them into English, so the book contains both versions. All thirty-one drawings and entries are collected in the attached PDF, in order. Content note: the story contains violence, psychological cruelty, captivity and bodily harm. Please read it with care.
